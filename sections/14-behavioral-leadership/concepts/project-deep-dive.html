1Pick the right flagship project (the L5 scope test)

Most senior candidates pick their most recent project instead of their most defensible project. Score your top 3 past projects from 1 to 3 on each row below—your highest scorer is your flagship:

2The 60-second "Tell me about yourself"

Interviewers use this opener to calibrate your seniority in the first minute. Keep it strictly under 60 seconds using Present → Past → Future:

1. PRESENT (15s): Role, team scope, and the core systems you own today.
2. PAST    (30s): 1–2 defining achievements with real numbers (tease your flagship project).
3. FUTURE  (15s): Why this level & type of problem now (tailored to Google / Anthropic / target).
Say it out loud with a stopwatch until it feels conversational—never memorized like a script, never a 4-minute chronological resume reading.

3How to deliver the 5-minute architecture map

In a past-project deep dive (common in Anthropic HM/technical screens and Google team-match/G&L probes), never dump 20 minutes of monologue. Give a tight 5-minute zoomable overview and invite the interviewer to pull on any thread:

  1. Context & pain (45s): What product, what broke or scaled poorly, and the baseline number.
  2. 4–7 box diagram (2m): Draw the request/data flow cleanly with approximate QPS, data size, and latency targets.
  3. Your 2 hardest trade-offs (1m 30s): State what you chose, what you rejected, and which constraint forced your hand.
  4. Measured result & invitation (45s): Give the before/after metric, then ask: "Which part would you like to dive deepest into—the storage layer, failure handling, or rollout?"
Saying "we" hides your level. Use "I" for decisions you made and credit teammates explicitly for what they owned—interviewers respect honesty and clarity about scope.

4Quick checks

An interviewer asks about your flagship migration and you say: "We decided to dual-write and we migrated traffic over two weeks." What signal does that accidentally hide?

Spot on. Give team context once ("three of us worked on this"), then switch cleanly to "I designed the shadow-traffic verifier and owned the cutover plan, while Sarah built the backfill workers."

Hiring committees at Google and Anthropic specifically scan behavioral notes for individual ownership. Using "we" throughout leaves the committee unable to distinguish L4 execution from L5 technical leadership.

Why do interviewers always ask "What alternatives did you reject, and what would you change now?" during a project deep dive?

Exactly. Especially at Anthropic (epistemic humility) and Google L5 (senior judgment), candidly explaining a trade-off that aged poorly is a strong hire signal.

Every real system has scars and trade-offs. Claiming a design had no downsides signals either junior scope or lack of self-awareness—naming the rejected alternative and the constraint that settled it proves real ownership.

In a 45-minute past-project deep dive, how long should your initial project overview take before pausing for questions?

Right! A 5-minute high-signal map proves you can summarize at executive altitude while leaving 35+ minutes for the deep technical grilling that earns a Strong Hire.

A 20-minute monologue burns half the round on areas the interviewer doesn't care about, while a 30-second opener forces them to drag context out of you. Aim for a tight 5-minute map and hand them the zoom lens.
